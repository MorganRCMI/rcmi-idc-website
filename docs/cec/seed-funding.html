Funding opportunities for community-academic partnerships pursuing Community-Based Participatory Research.

Overview

Community-academic research partnerships using Community Based Participatory Research (CBPR) principles are essential for addressing health disparities. CBPR emphasizes equitable partnerships with communities, recognizing strengths and resources, treating them as a unit of identity. Successful CBPR entails building trust, balancing power, negotiating roles, and promoting shared governance. These partnerships create local ecosystems for networking, resource sharing, capacity building and mentorship. Engaging diverse leaders fosters co-learning and knowledge-sharing.

With this in mind, Morgan CARES offers two seed funding programs to support the formation, maintenance, and success of collaborative research partnerships. With access to the Morgan CARES infrastructure, these partnerships can have lasting impact on community health.

Community Award Program

Overview

The Morgan CARES Community Award is a small seed funding initiative intended to support community-campus collaborations. The goal of this small award is to promote and strengthen partnerships between community-based organizations and groups, and members of academia at Morgan State University. It is our hope that these connections form a strong foundation that supports the emergence of responsive, community-led public health initiatives that promote health equity in communities across Baltimore City. New partners are encouraged to participate in Morgan CARES training, consultations, networking opportunities, and other supportive activities for as long as needed before developing and submitting an application. Existing community-campus partnerships are also eligible to apply.

Funding for new partnerships is offered every other year. During each funding cycle (FC), Morgan CARES provides up to five (5) Community Awards of $2,000 to support the development and implementation of collaborative partnership activities. Project teams that successfully complete their initial $2,000 award and remain in good standing and actively engaged in their partnership are eligible to apply for a competitive $5,000 Community Award Continuation to further expand or build upon their work. Morgan CARES is able to fund up to two (2) continuation awards during each funding cycle.

Practitioner Research Award Program

Overview

The Morgan CARES Practitioner Research Award is a small seed funding initiative intended to support collaborative teams working on health-related projects and initiatives that are clinically-oriented or practice-based. The goal of this small award is to promote and strengthen collaboration, and to ultimately generate knowledge that will improve clinical care in various community-centered settings. It is intended that the connections formed during this seed award provide a strong foundation that supports the emergence of responsive public health initiatives that address a practice problem and promote health in communities across Baltimore City.

New partnerships may engage in capacity building activities and trainings hosted by Morgan CARES. Those potential collaborators can participate in those activities for as long as they wish, to forge a strong interdisciplinary partnership before developing and submitting an application. Interdisciplinary partner teams who have existing collaborations may also apply.

Morgan CARES is able to support up to 2 Practitioner Research Award projects a year, and applications are accepted on a rolling basis. Applications are reviewed each quarter, and projects are selected for funding. Applications will continue to be accepted while funds remain available.

New Applicants

Eligible applications must…

  • Each team must have at least one representative (a Faculty Member or Doctoral-level student) from a Baltimore-area academic institution. Each team must also include an individual with clinical practice knowledge or experience that also has an organizational affiliation.
  • A practitioner refers to a:
    • Health Practitioner (e.g. physician or nurse)
    • Community-based provider (e.g. case manager or doula)
    • Allied Health Professional (e.g. physical therapy or occupational therapy)
    • Public Health Practitioner
    • Social Worker
    • Individual in Behavioral & Mental Health Services
    • Individual in Health Administration or Health Services Management
  • Have a project timeline of 1 year or less.
  • Identify a practice problem that is impacting service delivery, access, or provision.
  • Propose a collaborative health initiative that explores or seeks to better understand a practice-related problem, tests a solution or conducts an evaluation.
  • Proposals should result in improvements in health practice, delivery, access, programming or policy that directly impacts a patient population or focal community within Baltimore City.
  • Demonstrate a clear use of strategies, approaches, and experiences from a specified discipline (such as nursing, social work, case management, etc.).
  • Demonstrate a commitment to utilizing the data and other products from this small award to disseminate the results, and grow and expand the project and partnership.
  • Include a budget that is not more than $5,000, utilizing the provided template.
