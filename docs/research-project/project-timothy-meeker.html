Neural and Immune Correlates of Painful Chemotherapy-Induced Neuropathy, Feasibility and Preliminary Efficacy of a Motor Cortex Non-Invasive Brain Stimulation Intervention

A randomized controlled trial testing non-invasive brain stimulation (tDCS) as a treatment for painful chemotherapy-induced neuropathy, examining differences in pain response and brain function between Non-Hispanic Black and Non-Hispanic White patients.

Over half of cancer patients receiving common chemotherapy treatments experience painful nerve damage called chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy (CIPN). Non-Hispanic Black patients are more likely to suffer from this condition and more often need to reduce their chemotherapy doses compared to Non-Hispanic White patients. This RCMI@Morgan Research Project (Project 3) is conducting a double-blind, randomized controlled trial testing transcranial direct current stimulation (tDCS) of the motor cortex — a safe, at-home, non-invasive brain stimulation intervention — as a treatment for painful CIPN. The study aims to (1) determine the feasibility and efficacy of active tDCS as an analgesic and antihyperalgesic intervention, (2) identify structural and functional brain correlates of painful CIPN in Non-Hispanic Black and Non-Hispanic White patients, and (3) examine inflammatory mediators linked to CIPN severity and brain function, in support of RCMI@Morgan's mission to address urban health disparities in Baltimore.
