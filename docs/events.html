What It Is

A guided path from proposal idea to NIH-style structure

This workshop will teach individuals with no prior NIH funding or grant experience how to design an NIH-style scientific research proposal focused on basic biomedical research or health disparities research.

Workshop Experience

Participants will work through a four-week online summer workshop focused on basic biomedical research or health disparities research. The format combines a two-day Zoom kickoff, four weekly synchronous Zoom meetings, and weekly assignments between meetings.

Accepted applicants will complete pre-workshop tasks, submit weekly assignments, and complete a final product by the end of the workshop.

  • Compose a concise, testable research question focused on basic biomedical research or health disparities research.
  • Delineate the significance and innovative aspects of the research question, including short- or long-term health benefits.
  • Support the research question with a review of relevant research literature.
  • Construct a study design that appropriately tests the research question.
  • Negotiate federal grant information and identify programs of interest.

New This Year

Community Partners Welcome

We are now inviting community members who plan to partner with MSU faculty on Community Based Participatory Research (CBPR) grant applications. This is a new pathway — apply alongside early-stage MSU investigators.

Early-Stage MSU Investigators

New tenure-track faculty or postdocs working in biomedical, translational, behavioral health sciences, or other public health related fields.

First-Time Federal Funding

Applicants should not have previously received an RCMI Pilot Project Program award or federal funding agency support.

Proposal Timeline

Applicants should intend to submit a grant proposal within the next year.

Application Form
